# Visual language

What ALIS footage should feel like, and why. This owns the style; the
[shot board](../../scripts/ue/cinematic/shots/README.md) owns the numbers that
enforce it, and [raw_capture.md](raw_capture.md) owns the machinery.

## The promise

Every piece of ALIS footage is making one claim:

> These are real places, rebuilt at their real size, and you can move through them.

Everything else is in service of that. A frame that is beautiful but does not
advance the claim is decoration, and decoration is what gets cut first.

## The viewer

Assume someone who has never heard of ALIS, watching without sound, deciding in
under ten seconds whether to keep watching. Three consequences, and they are not
negotiable:

- **Whatever must land, lands early.** Do not save the best frame for the end.
- **Nothing depends on narration.** If a shot needs a caption to mean anything,
  the shot is not doing its job.
- **Comprehension beats impressiveness.** A move the viewer cannot follow
  communicates nothing, however dramatic it looks.

Before any shot exists, it answers one question in a sentence: *what will a
stranger understand from this?* A shot without an answer does not get captured.

## Honesty

This is the line the style will not cross.

- Film what the product actually does. Never stage a capability the build does
  not have, and never imply one through framing or edit.
- Do not hide a known defect by pretending it is a choice. Frame away from it
  honestly, or accept it in shot.
- A cinematic render is not a claim about runtime performance. It says the world
  exists and is this large; it does not say it runs this way on your machine.

Ugly truth beats a pretty lie. The footage is evidence, and evidence that
overstates is worth less than none.

## Shooting to what ALIS is

The reconstruction's strength is **extent, density and silhouette**: thousands of
correctly placed, correctly sized volumes across kilometres of real terrain, with
rivers and relief where the real city has them. Its current weakness is
**surface**: the massing carries no facades.

That single fact decides the whole visual language.

- **Distance is the friend.** At medium aerial distance the city reads as a city; up
  close it reads as untextured slabs. What distance that actually is depends on the
  renderer's loaded envelope, so the capture route decides the number and this
  document only insists on the principle.
- **Silhouette over surface.** Compose for skyline shape, block rhythm and the
  cut of a street grid, not for material.
- **The white massing is a look, not a failure.** It reads as a precise
  architectural model, which is honest about what has been rebuilt. Do not
  apologise for it, and do not shoot as though facades were coming.
- **The one close exception is speed.** In a corridor flight, near geometry
  passing fast is the subject, and it works because nobody reads surface at that
  velocity.

## Composition

- **Three depth layers.** Foreground, midground, background. Two layers is a
  postcard; three is a world.
- **Give scale something to measure against.** A river, a bridge, a park, a
  recognisable tower. A dense field with no reference reads as texture, not as a
  city the size of a city.
- **Water enriches a wide.** City edge to edge goes flat. Water in the upper third
  gives the frame a horizon it has earned.
- **Never frame the whole territory from outside it.** The tile has hard edges;
  seen whole it becomes a diorama on a table, which is the exact opposite of the
  claim.
- **Dead space is a decision.** A little empty sky or water is composition. A lot
  is a wasted frame.

## Motion

Motion is the argument. A static wide could be a photograph; only relative motion
between near and far proves the depth is real.

Each move says something different, and the shot should be chosen for what it
needs to say:

| Move | What it argues |
|---|---|
| push | this subject matters, look at it |
| traverse | the world is continuous, not one viewpoint |
| descent | here is what that scale means at human size |
| orbit | this thing is built on every side |
| corridor | you are inside it, and it is fast |
| reveal | there is more here than you were shown |

Two standing rules follow from comprehension: **slow enough to read**, and **a
move must arrive somewhere**. If the end frame teaches nothing the start frame
did not, the motion was decoration.

Straight, level travel is the weakest possible move: flat parallax, nothing
changing but position. Travel diagonally and change altitude, so near and far
geometry shear against each other and the eye keeps working.

## Rhythm

- Capture long, cut short. Fifteen to thirty seconds per take with a still hold at
  each end, so an editor can trim, dissolve, retime or let a shot breathe.
- Vary between takes. Different altitude, lens or direction each time. Eight
  similar drone shots are one shot repeated eight times.
- Open on proof, not on branding.
- Leave the assembly to a person. Raw material is the deliverable.

## What we do not do

- No audio, titles, captions or voice-over in raw capture.
- No motion the viewer cannot follow.
- No shot that requires explanation to mean anything.
- No implied capability the build does not have.
- No close-up on massing outside a corridor flight.
- No whole-territory framing.
